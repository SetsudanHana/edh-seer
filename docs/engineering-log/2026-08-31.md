## A modal DFC was paid for twice, in opposite directions, on the same screen

Owner report, from the deployed site: *"there is one confusing part on the UI where in one place we
lower the expected land count by amount of mdfcs and on the other we use mdfcs as land, and for Rani
we print Lands 38 — high, aim for 33."*

Reproduced on `enchanting-rani` before anything moved:

| number | value |
|---|---|
| type-line land count, which `build.ts` scores and reports | **38** |
| `landRec.actual`, which held MDFC land-backs OUT | **34** |
| `landRec.target`, already discounted for those MDFCs | **33** |
| MDFC land-backs | 3 untapped · 1 tapped |

**The same four cards were added to the count and subtracted from the target: +4 in one place and
−2.60 in the other (3 × 0.74 + 1 × 0.38), a phantom surplus of 6.6 lands.** The deck read five over
when the honest pair is 38 against 36.

`analyze.ts:877` hands `computeBuild` the discounted `landRec.target` while `build.ts:467` counts
lands by type line. It was never only cosmetic: `build.ts:525` computes `attainment` from that same
pair, so every deck running an MDFC had been docked build score for cards it owns.

**`deck-math.ts` was already internally consistent** — it pairs `rec.actual` with `rec.target`, and
`BuildBenchmarks.tsx` rendered the reconciliation: *"34 (38 with MDFCs) · 4 modal DFCs priced as
spells, discounting the target rather than counting as lands."* Its own comment had predicted this
report — *"Two numbers on one screen with no explanation reads as a defect in the report"* — and the
explanation existed on the panel while the gaps list printed the raw pair with nothing beside it.

### The ruling, and why it went against the published regression

Three conventions were measured across the **55 of 71 calibration decks that run a land-back MDFC**,
as delta between the deck's land count and its target:

| convention | mean delta | mean \|delta\| | outside ±3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| as it was — discount applied, card still counted | **+1.56** | 2.58 | 17/55 (15 high, 2 low) |
| all-Karsten — count it as a spell, keep the discount | **−1.40** | 2.53 | 16/55 (3 high, **13 low**) |
| **an MDFC is a land, no discount** | **−0.36** | 2.18 | **12/55 (6 high, 6 low)** |

**The shipped arm wins on all three, and it is the only one whose misses are symmetric.** The
all-Karsten arm reading real decks 1.4 lands SHORT is the substantive finding: that is the signature
of players already treating these as lands and running fewer real ones to compensate, which is
exactly the owner's argument — *"typically you play mdfcs as a land primarily and then you can use
the spell if you have enough nonmdfc lands"*. **The first recommendation from this seat was the
all-Karsten arm, and the measurement reversed it.**

CHECKED, NOT ASSUMED: every tapped classification across the 71 decks is the always-tapped cycle —
12 distinct cards, all reading *"This land enters tapped."* (Malakir Mire, Khalni Territory, Valakut
Stoneforge, Kazuul's Cliffs, Silundi Isle …). The pay-3-life rares all classify untapped, so
`mdfcLandBack` already draws the line the owner's second point named.

**COST, NAMED RATHER THAN HIDDEN:** 0.74-vs-0.38 was the model's way of saying a tapped land-back is
worth half an untapped one, and counting both at 1.0 throws that away. `mdfcUntapped`/`mdfcTapped`
are still reported so a refinement has its input; inventing a third coefficient to split the
difference would be fitting to 55 decks.

### The other half of the double count

A card counted as a land also has to leave the pool that sets `avgManaValue` — the regression's
dominant term — or its mana value pushes the target back up by part of what the count just gained.
Small (~0.03 across the calibration decks) and wrong in principle; it has its own test. It is also
why five decks' targets moved DOWN: their MDFCs are expensive, so the spell pool got cheaper.

## Mobile is its own round now, and the graph is absent by construction

Owner, from a phone: *"I can not see the graph"*, and *"on cards list there is top item on the list
cut."*

The first is not a rendering bug. `ReportTabs.tsx:126` branches on `useIsNarrow()` and renders
`GraphList` — the graph's DATA as a list — instead of `GraphView`, the canvas; `use-narrow.ts` puts
the cutoff at Tailwind's `sm`, 639px, **so every phone in portrait gets the list and never the
board.** Owner ruled the board itself has to work on a phone, deferred to a testing-and-planning
round. **The blocker there is INPUT, not size**: hover carries the tooltip and touch has no hover.

The second has a prime suspect and is not yet reproduced: `CardList.tsx:336` sticks the table header
at a hardcoded `top-[33px]` offsetting the tab strip, and that strip carries `overflow-x-auto`
specifically because at 390px its five tabs run 32px past the row. Step one is measuring the strip's
real height against the 33 the header assumes; the fix deletes the magic number rather than re-tuning
it.
